# Tickets

Holding $LOOT puts tickets in the box. More tickets, better odds. There's nothing to buy or register.

## The rule

> Every **{per} $LOOT** of your average balance during a drop is **one ticket**. Fractions round down.

Your average is taken across several **snapshots**: readings of every $LOOT balance on Solana, taken at random times nobody knows in advance.

<Rule rule="snapshots" />

Your chance of winning a drop is your tickets divided by all tickets in the box.

## Why an average

If tickets were based on one moment, someone could buy a huge bag right before the draw and sell right after. Averaging across unannounced snapshots means a last-minute buy only counts in the snapshots that come after it.

## Example

Six snapshots are taken during a drop.

| Wallet | Holds | Average | Tickets |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 50,000 $LOOT the whole time | 50,000 | **5** |
| B | 0, then buys 100,000 before the last snapshot | 16,666 | **1** |
| C | 9,000 the whole time | 9,000 | **0**, under one ticket |
| D | 250,000, sells half after snapshot 3 | 187,500 | **18** |

## Good to know

- **One wallet is never worse than several.** Odds are proportional, so splitting doesn't help, and rounding down in each wallet can only lose you fractions.
- **Every drop counts separately.** Each drop has its own snapshots. If you keep holding, you're in every drop.
- **Selling lowers your balance in every snapshot after the sale.** Tickets from earlier snapshots still count toward your average.
- **Some wallets can never win.** The pump.fun bonding curve, the liquidity pool, the team and dev wallets, and the treasury are excluded. Their addresses are listed on [Addresses](/docs/addresses).
- **Only wallets someone holds the key to get tickets.** An account run by a program, such as another liquidity pool or a vault, can't sign a claim, so it's left out of every snapshot.
- **Check your tickets** any time from the "Check your tickets" link on the [live page](/).
