# Tickets

Holding $LOOT puts tickets in the box, and more tickets means better odds. There's nothing to buy and nothing to sign up for.

## The rule

> Every **{per} $LOOT** of your average balance during a drop is **one ticket**. Fractions round down.

Your average comes from several **snapshots**. A snapshot records every $LOOT balance on Solana at a random time nobody knows in advance.

<Rule rule="snapshots" />

Your chance of winning a drop is your tickets divided by all the tickets in the box.

## Why an average

If tickets came from a single moment, someone could buy a big bag right before the draw and sell right after. Averaging over unannounced snapshots means a last-minute buy only counts in the snapshots taken after it.

## Example

Six snapshots are taken during a drop.

| Wallet | Holds | Average | Tickets |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 50,000 $LOOT the whole time | 50,000 | **5** |
| B | 0, then buys 100,000 before the last snapshot | 16,666 | **1** |
| C | 9,000 the whole time | 9,000 | **0**, under one ticket |
| D | 250,000, sells half after snapshot 3 | 187,500 | **18** |

## Other details

- Splitting your $LOOT across wallets doesn't help. Odds are proportional, and each wallet rounds down on its own, so splitting can only lose you fractions.
- Each drop has its own snapshots. If you keep holding, you're in every drop.
- Selling lowers your balance in the snapshots after the sale. Earlier snapshots still count toward your average.
- Some wallets can never win: the pump.fun bonding curve, the liquidity pool, the team and dev wallets, and the treasury. Their addresses are on [Addresses](/docs/addresses).
- Accounts run by a program, such as another liquidity pool or a vault, are left out of every snapshot because they can't sign a claim.
- You can look up a wallet in the **Your tickets** box on the [live page](/).
